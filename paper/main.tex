\title[Stability thresholds of fake weighted projective spaces]{Stability
thresholds of log pairs on fake weighted projective spaces and
K\"ahler--Einstein calibration}

\date{July 2, 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We give a closed formula for the stability threshold (\(\delta\)-invariant)
of an arbitrary log Fano pair \((X,\Delta)\), where \(X\) is a fake weighted
projective space with weights \((a_0,\dots,a_n)\) and
\(\Delta=\sum_i c_i D_i\) is a torus-invariant boundary: writing
\(\beta_i = 1-c_i\),
\[
\delta(X,\Delta) \;=\; \frac{(n+1)\,\min_i a_i\beta_i}{\sum_i a_i\beta_i},
\]
and the infimum is computed by the divisorial valuation of a boundary
divisor minimizing \(a_i\beta_i\). In particular \(\delta(X,\Delta)\le 1\)
always: no such pair is uniformly K-stable, and \((X,\Delta)\) is
K-semistable if and only if the products \(a_i\beta_i\) are all equal, in
which case it is K-polystable and admits a conical K\"ahler--Einstein
metric. Consequently every fake weighted projective space carries a
canonical one-parameter family of \emph{calibrating} boundaries making it a
K-polystable log Fano pair; the threshold is independent of the fundamental
group of the smooth locus; and the alpha invariant saturates the lower
Blum--Jonsson bound, \(\alpha = \delta/(n+1)\). The proof combines the
toric formula of Blum--Jonsson for \(S\)-invariants with Zhuang's theorem
on equivariant computation of stability thresholds of K-unstable pairs,
and yields an explicit chamber decomposition of the coefficient cube by
optimal destabilizing divisors. We relate the result to recent work on
delta invariants of weighted hypersurfaces and on wall crossings for
K-moduli of weighted projective pairs. All formulas have been verified
independently by exact-arithmetic computer computation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The theory of K-stability of Fano varieties has undergone a spectacular
development in the last decade. On the foundational side, the resolution of
the Yau--Tian--Donaldson conjecture \cite{CDS15,Tia15} and its extension to
singular Fano varieties \cite{LXZ22} established the equivalence between
K-polystability and the existence of K\"ahler--Einstein metrics, while the
algebraic construction of projective K-moduli spaces of log Fano pairs was
completed through the work of many authors (see the surveys
\cite{Xu21,Xu24}); a new, purely birational proof of the properness of these
moduli spaces has very recently been given by Blum, Liu, Xu and Zhuang
\cite{BLXZ25}. On the computational side, the stability threshold, or
\(\delta\)-invariant, of Fujita--Odaka \cite{FO18} and Blum--Jonsson
\cite{BJ20}, together with the method of Abban--Zhuang \cite{AZ22}, has made
it possible to decide K-stability for large explicit classes of Fano
varieties. Two very recent directions motivate this note:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Weighted hypersurfaces.} Sano--Tasin \cite{ST24} proved the
lower bound \(\delta(\mathcal O_X(1)) \ge (n+1)a_r/d\) for quasi-smooth
weighted hypersurfaces \(X_d\subset\PP(a_0,\dots,a_{n+1})\) whenever some
weight \(a_r>1\) divides \(d\), and Campo--Fujita--Sano--Tasin
\cite{CFST26} developed a general framework of plt flags and convex
geometry which, among other things, settles K-stability of all quasi-smooth
Fano weighted hypersurfaces of index \(1\) with at most two weights greater
than one.
\item \emph{K-moduli of weighted projective pairs.} Kim--Liu--Wang
\cite{KLW24} described wall crossings for K-moduli of hypersurfaces in
\(\PP(1,2,n+2,n+3)\) by reducing to log Fano pairs
\((\PP(1,2,n+2),\,cD)\) and estimating stability thresholds of such pairs
with the Abban--Zhuang method; explicit K-moduli descriptions for Fano
threefold families in the same spirit appear in \cite{EJP25,LZ24}.
\end{itemize}

In both directions the ambient objects are weighted projective spaces, or
more generally their finite quotients, decorated with torus-invariant
boundaries. It is therefore useful to have a \emph{closed formula} for the
stability threshold of an arbitrary such pair, valid without any genericity
or smoothability hypothesis. The purpose of this paper is to record this
formula, its short conceptual proof, and several consequences that do not
seem to have appeared in the literature in this generality.

Recall that a \emph{fake weighted projective space} is a \(\Q\)-factorial
projective toric variety of Picard number one \cite{Buc08,Kas09}. Such a
variety is determined by \(n+1\) primitive lattice vectors
\(v_0,\dots,v_n\) spanning \(\NR\simeq\R^n\) and satisfying a relation
\(\sum_i a_i v_i = 0\) with positive integral coprime \emph{weights}
\(a_i\); it is a quotient \(X = \PP(a_0,\dots,a_n)/G\) of a weighted
projective space by a finite abelian group acting freely in codimension
one. Let \(D_0,\dots,D_n\) denote the torus-invariant prime divisors,
corresponding to the rays \(\R_{\ge0}v_i\).

\begin{theoremA}[= Theorem \ref{thm:main}]
Let \(X\) be a fake weighted projective space of dimension \(n\) with
weights \((a_0,\dots,a_n)\), and let
\(\Delta=\sum_{i=0}^{n} c_i D_i\) with rational \(0\le c_i<1\). Set
\(\beta_i := 1-c_i\) and \(B:=\sum_{i} a_i\beta_i\). Then \((X,\Delta)\) is
a log Fano pair, and its stability threshold equals
\[
\delta(X,\Delta)\;=\;\frac{(n+1)\min_{0\le i\le n} a_i\beta_i}{B}\,,
\]
computed by the divisorial valuation \(\ord_{D_{i_0}}\) for any index
\(i_0\) minimizing \(a_i\beta_i\). Moreover
\(\alpha(X,\Delta) = \delta(X,\Delta)/(n+1) = \min_i a_i \beta_i / B\).
\end{theoremA}

Since \((n+1)\min_i a_i\beta_i \le \sum_i a_i\beta_i\), with equality if
and only if all the products \(a_i\beta_i\) coincide, Theorem A immediately
yields:

\begin{corollaryB}[= Corollary \ref{cor:kss}]
With notation as above:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \(\delta(X,\Delta)\le 1\); in particular no log pair supported on
the toric boundary of a fake weighted projective space is uniformly
K-stable.
\item \((X,\Delta)\) is K-semistable if and only if
\(a_0\beta_0=a_1\beta_1=\dots=a_n\beta_n\), if and only if the barycenter
of the moment polytope of \(-(K_X+\Delta)\) is the origin. In this case
\((X,\Delta)\) is K-polystable and admits a K\"ahler--Einstein metric with
cone angle \(2\pi\beta_i\) along \(D_i\).
\item For \(\Delta=0\): a fake weighted projective space is K-semistable
if and only if all its weights are equal, i.e.\ if and only if it is a
quotient \(\PP^n/G\) by a finite abelian group acting freely in
codimension one.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollaryB}

Item (iii) recovers, and makes effective, the barycenter criterion of
Berman \cite{Ber16} in this class of examples, and complements the
necessary condition of Hwang--Yoon \cite{HY22} for a fake weighted
projective space to be K\"ahler--Einstein. The equality case in (ii) has
the following consequence, which we call \emph{K\"ahler--Einstein
calibration}: unstable weights can always be repaired, in a unique
one-parameter fashion, by a boundary.

\begin{corollaryC}[= Corollary \ref{cor:calibration}]
Let \(X\) be a fake weighted projective space with weights
\((a_0,\dots,a_n)\) and set \(a_{\min}:=\min_i a_i\). For every
\(t\in(0,a_{\min}]\cap\Q\) the pair
\[
\Bigl(X,\;\Delta(t)\Bigr),\qquad
\Delta(t) := \sum_{i=0}^n \Bigl(1-\frac{t}{a_i}\Bigr) D_i ,
\]
is a K-polystable log Fano pair, and these are the \emph{only}
torus-invariant boundaries on \(X\) with this property. In particular
every fake weighted projective space admits a canonical conical
K\"ahler--Einstein structure.
\end{corollaryC}

Two further consequences are worth highlighting. First, the formula in
Theorem A depends only on the weights and not on the lattice; hence the
stability threshold of a fake weighted projective space agrees with that of
its weighted projective universal cover (Corollary \ref{cor:lattice}),
even though the volume drops by the index of the covering. Second, the
alpha and delta invariants of these pairs saturate the lower bound in the
Blum--Jonsson inequality
\(\delta/(n+1)\le\alpha\le n\delta/(n+1)\) \cite{BJ20}, so fake weighted
projective pairs are extremal for this inequality in all dimensions
(Corollary \ref{cor:alpha}); via
\(R(X,\Delta)=\min\{1,\delta(X,\Delta)\}\) \cite{CRZ19,BBJ21} one also
obtains the greatest Ricci lower bound of all these pairs.

The proof of Theorem A is short but, we believe, instructive. The upper
bound is an explicit computation with the barycenter formula of
Blum--Jonsson for \(S\)-invariants of toric valuations \cite{BJ20}. The
lower bound \emph{does not} require the (hard) toric reduction of
\cite[Theorem F]{BJ20}: instead we invoke Zhuang's equivariant theorem
\cite{Zhu21}, which says that the stability threshold of a K-\emph{unstable}
log Fano pair is computed by \(\Aut\)-invariant divisors that are lc places
of complements; on a toric pair, torus-invariant divisorial valuations are
monomial, so K-instability forces \(\delta = \delta^T\), the toric
threshold. Since the toric threshold never exceeds \(1\) in our situation,
this single dichotomy proves the theorem in all cases at once. As a
by-product we obtain an explicit chamber decomposition of the coefficient
cube \([0,1)^{n+1}\) into \(n+1\) regions on which the \emph{optimal
destabilizing divisor}, in the sense of \cite{Zhu21,BLXZ25}, is the
corresponding boundary divisor \(D_i\); the K-semistable locus is exactly
the common wall (Section \ref{sec:examples}).

All numerical assertions of this paper (the \(S\)-invariant formulas, the
value of \(\delta\) on families of examples, the barycenter computations,
and the equality \(\alpha=\delta/(n+1)\)) have been verified independently
by exact rational-arithmetic computations: finite-level approximations
\(S_m\) computed from monomial bases of \(H^0(X,-m(K_X+\Delta))\) converge
to the claimed values, and the infimum of \(A/S\) over several thousand
randomly sampled toric valuations is attained at the predicted ray, for a
range of weight systems, quotient lattices and boundary coefficients. The
verification script is distributed with the source of this article.

\subsection*{Relation to the literature} For \(\Delta=0\) and
\(X=\PP(a_0,\dots,a_n)\) the formula
\(\delta = (n+1)a_{\min}/\sum a_i\) is known to experts; it follows,
for instance, from \cite[Corollary 7.19]{BJ20} after a short computation
(see Remark \ref{rem:known}). The contribution of this paper is: the
general boundary version on arbitrary fake weighted projective spaces
with its uniform one-line formula; the calibration corollary; the
lattice-independence statement; the saturation of the Blum--Jonsson
inequality; and the chamber decomposition by optimal destabilizers, which
gives closed-form baselines for wall-crossing analyses such as
\cite{KLW24,ADL24}. We emphasize that we make no smoothability assumption,
which is essential for applications to K-moduli of non-smoothable Fano
varieties as in \cite{KLW24}.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

We work over \(\C\). A \emph{log Fano pair} \((X,\Delta)\) consists of a
normal projective variety \(X\) and an effective \(\Q\)-divisor \(\Delta\)
such that \((X,\Delta)\) is klt and \(-(K_X+\Delta)\) is \(\Q\)-Cartier
and ample.

\subsection{Stability thresholds}\label{ss:delta}
Let \((X,\Delta)\) be a log Fano pair of dimension \(n\) and set
\(L:=-(K_X+\Delta)\). For a prime divisor \(E\) over \(X\) (i.e.\ a prime
divisor on some normal birational model \(Y\to X\)), let
\(A_{X,\Delta}(E)\) denote the log discrepancy and
\[
S_{X,\Delta}(E) \;=\; S_L(E) \;:=\; \frac{1}{\Vol(L)}\int_0^{\infty}
\Vol(\pi^*L - tE)\,dt
\]
the expected vanishing order. Both quantities extend by homogeneity and
continuity to valuations. Following \cite{FO18,BJ20} the \emph{stability
threshold} (or \(\delta\)-invariant) and the \emph{alpha invariant} are
\[
\delta(X,\Delta) := \inf_{E}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E)}{S_L(E)},
\qquad
\alpha(X,\Delta) := \inf_{E}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E)}{T_L(E)},
\]
where \(T_L(E):=\sup\{t>0 : \pi^*L-tE \text{ big}\}\) and the infima run
over all prime divisors over \(X\). By \cite{FO18,BJ20} (building on
\cite{Fuj19,Li17}), \((X,\Delta)\) is K-semistable iff
\(\delta(X,\Delta)\ge1\) and uniformly K-stable iff
\(\delta(X,\Delta)>1\); by \cite{LXZ22} uniform K-stability coincides with
K-stability. The elementary chain of pointwise inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ST}
\frac{1}{\,n+1\,}\,T_L(E)\;\le\; S_L(E)\;\le\;\frac{n}{\,n+1\,}\,T_L(E)
\end{equation}
(see \cite[\S3]{BJ20}) yields
\(\delta/(n+1)\le\alpha\le n\,\delta/(n+1)\).

We will use the following two results. The first is the equivariant
computation theorem of Zhuang. For the notion of \emph{lc place of a
complement} see \cite[\S2]{Zhu21}; we only need that the class of
\(G\)-invariant such divisors is a subclass of all \(G\)-invariant prime
divisors over \(X\).

\begin{theorem}[{Zhuang \cite[Theorem 1.2]{Zhu21}}]\label{thm:zhuang}
Let \((X,\Delta)\) be a log Fano pair with \(G=\Aut(X,\Delta)\), and
assume \((X,\Delta)\) is \emph{not} K-semistable. Then
\[
\delta(X,\Delta) = \inf_E \frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E)}{S_L(E)},
\]
where the infimum runs over all \(G\)-invariant prime divisors \(E\) over
\(X\) that are lc places of complements.
\end{theorem}

The second is the polytope formula for \(S\)-invariants of toric
valuations, due to Blum--Jonsson.

\subsection{Toric valuations}\label{ss:toric}
Let \(N\simeq\Z^n\) be a lattice, \(M\) its dual, and \(X=X_\Sigma\) a
complete toric variety with fan \(\Sigma\subset\NR\) and open torus
\(T\). Every \(w\in \NR\setminus\{0\}\) determines a \emph{monomial (toric)
valuation} \(v_w\) on \(\C(X)=\C(M)\), defined on the group algebra by
\(v_w\bigl(\sum_u c_u\chi^u\bigr)=\min_{c_u\neq0}\langle u,w\rangle\); if
\(w=v_i\) is a primitive ray generator then \(v_w=\ord_{D_i}\). Given an
ample \(\Q\)-line bundle \(L\) with moment polytope
\(P=P_L\subset\MReal\), Blum--Jonsson prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BJS}
S_L(v_w) \;=\; \langle \bary, w\rangle \;-\;\min_{u\in P}\langle u,
w\rangle,
\qquad
T_L(v_w) \;=\; \max_{u\in P}\langle u,w\rangle-\min_{u\in P}\langle
u,w\rangle,
\end{equation}
where \(\bary\) is the barycenter of \(P\)
\cite[Corollary 7.7]{BJ20}. If moreover \(\Delta=\sum_i c_iD_i\) is a
torus-invariant boundary with \(c_i<1\), then \((X,\Delta)\) is
automatically klt, and the log discrepancy of a monomial valuation is
given by the piecewise-linear function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Atoric}
A_{X,\Delta}(v_w) = \varphi_\Delta(w), \qquad \text{where }
\varphi_\Delta \text{ is linear on each cone of } \Sigma \text{ and }
\varphi_\Delta(v_i)=1-c_i
\end{equation}
(see e.g.\ \cite[\S7]{BJ20} for \(\Delta=0\); the general case follows
from \(A_{X,\Delta}(v_w)=A_X(v_w)-v_w(\Delta)\) and
\(v_w(D_i)=\lambda_i\) for \(w=\sum\lambda_i v_i\) in a cone containing
\(v_i\)).

We define the \emph{toric threshold}
\[
\delta^T(X,\Delta) := \inf_{w\in\NR\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v_w)}{S_L(v_w)} .
\]
Clearly \(\delta(X,\Delta)\le\delta^T(X,\Delta)\). The following classical
fact identifies torus-invariant divisorial valuations with monomial ones;
it goes back to the theory of invariant valuations on spherical varieties
\cite{Kno93} (see also \cite[\S7]{BJ20}).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:invariant}
Let \(X\) be a toric variety with torus \(T\), and let \(v\) be a
\(T\)-invariant divisorial valuation on \(\C(X)\). Then \(v=c\cdot v_w\)
for some \(w\in N_{\Q}\) and \(c\in\Q_{>0}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of proof]
A \(T\)-invariant valuation satisfies
\(v(f)=\min_u v(f_u)\) where \(f=\sum_u f_u\) is the decomposition into
\(T\)-eigenfunctions \(f_u\in\C^*\chi^u\) (see \cite{Kno93} or
\cite[\S19-20]{Tim11}). Since \(v\) is multiplicative on characters, the
assignment \(u\mapsto v(\chi^u)\) is additive, hence given by pairing with
a unique \(w'\in\NR\); then \(v=v_{w'}\) on all of \(\C(X)\) by the
displayed formula. Divisoriality forces \(w'\in N_\Q\) up to scaling.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fake weighted projective spaces}\label{ss:fwps}
Fix primitive vectors \(v_0,\dots,v_n\in N\) spanning \(\NR\) with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relation}
\sum_{i=0}^n a_i v_i = 0, \qquad a_i\in\Z_{>0},\quad
\gcd(a_0,\dots,a_n)=1 .
\end{equation}
The fan \(\Sigma\) whose cones are generated by the proper subsets of
\(\{v_0,\dots,v_n\}\) is complete and simplicial, and
\(X=X_\Sigma\) is a \(\Q\)-factorial projective toric variety of Picard
number one: a \emph{fake weighted projective space} with weights
\((a_0,\dots,a_n)\) \cite{Buc08,Kas09}. If \(N'\subset N\) denotes the
sublattice generated by \(v_0,\dots,v_n\), then \(X\) is the quotient of
\(\PP(a_0,\dots,a_n)\) (the case \(N'=N\)) by the dual group of \(N/N'\),
which acts freely in codimension one; the index \(m=[N:N']\) is called
the multiplicity of \(X\). Every effective nonzero \(\Q\)-divisor on
\(X\) is ample. We denote by \(D_i\) the torus-invariant prime divisor of
the ray \(\R_{\ge0} v_i\), so that \(-K_X=\sum_i D_i\).

For \(n=1\), primitivity and \eqref{eq:relation} force
\(v_0=-v_1\) and \(a_0=a_1=1\): the only fake weighted projective line is
\(\PP^1\). This is consistent with the classical fact that
one-dimensional weighted projective spaces are all isomorphic to
\(\PP^1\).

\begin{setup}\label{setup}
Throughout, \(X\) is a fake weighted projective space of dimension
\(n\ge1\) with weight--ray data \eqref{eq:relation};
\(\Delta=\sum_{i=0}^n c_iD_i\) with \(c_i\in[0,1)\cap\Q\);
\(\beta_i:=1-c_i\in(0,1]\); \(B:=\sum_i a_i\beta_i\); and
\(L:=-(K_X+\Delta)=\sum_i\beta_iD_i\), which is ample. The pair
\((X,\Delta)\) is klt, hence log Fano. The moment polytope of \(L\) is the
simplex
\[
P \;=\;\{u\in\MReal : \langle u,v_i\rangle\ge-\beta_i \ \text{for }
0\le i\le n\}.
\]
\end{setup}

\section{Proof of the main theorem}\label{sec:proof}

We begin with the elementary convex-geometric heart of the argument.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:vertex}
In Setup \textup{\ref{setup}}, \(P\) is a simplex with vertices
\(u_0,\dots,u_n\) characterized by
\(\langle u_j,v_i\rangle=-\beta_i\) for \(i\ne j\), and
\[
\langle u_j, v_j\rangle = \frac{B}{a_j}-\beta_j
\qquad (0\le j\le n).
\]
Consequently the barycenter \(\bary=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_j u_j\) of \(P\)
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barypairing}
\langle\bary, v_i\rangle = \frac{B}{(n+1)a_i}-\beta_i
\qquad (0\le i\le n).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(v_0,\dots,\widehat{v_j},\dots,v_n\) form a basis of \(\NR\)
for each \(j\), the \(n\) linear conditions
\(\langle u,v_i\rangle=-\beta_i\) \((i\ne j)\) determine a unique point
\(u_j\), and these are the vertices of the simplex \(P\). Pairing \(u_j\)
with the relation \eqref{eq:relation} gives
\[
0=\Bigl\langle u_j,\textstyle\sum_i a_iv_i\Bigr\rangle
= a_j\langle u_j,v_j\rangle-\sum_{i\ne j}a_i\beta_i
= a_j\langle u_j,v_j\rangle-(B-a_j\beta_j),
\]
whence \(\langle u_j,v_j\rangle = B/a_j-\beta_j\ge -\beta_j\) with strict
inequality (so \(u_j\in P\) and \(P\) is nondegenerate). Averaging the
pairings \(\langle u_j,v_i\rangle\) over \(j\) yields
\eqref{eq:barypairing}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Sray}
In Setup \textup{\ref{setup}}, for each \(i\),
\[
S_L(\ord_{D_i}) \;=\; \frac{B}{(n+1)\,a_i},
\qquad
T_L(\ord_{D_i}) \;=\; \frac{B}{a_i},
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(\ord_{D_i})}{S_L(\ord_{D_i})}
=\frac{(n+1)\,a_i\beta_i}{B}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:BJS} and Lemma \ref{lem:vertex},
\(\min_{u\in P}\langle u,v_i\rangle=-\beta_i\) (attained on the facet
opposite \(u_i\)) and
\(\max_{u\in P}\langle u,v_i\rangle=\langle u_i,v_i\rangle=B/a_i-\beta_i\),
so
\(S_L(\ord_{D_i})=\langle\bary,v_i\rangle+\beta_i=B/((n+1)a_i)\)
and \(T_L(\ord_{D_i})=B/a_i\). Finally
\(A_{X,\Delta}(\ord_{D_i})=1-c_i=\beta_i\) by \eqref{eq:Atoric}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Toric threshold]\label{prop:toric}
In Setup \textup{\ref{setup}},
\[
\delta^T(X,\Delta)=\frac{(n+1)\min_i a_i\beta_i}{B},
\]
and the infimum defining \(\delta^T\) is attained at \(w=v_{i_0}\) for any
\(i_0\) minimizing \(a_i\beta_i\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a maximal cone \(\sigma_j=\mathrm{cone}(v_i : i\ne j)\) of
\(\Sigma\). For \(w=\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_iv_i\in\sigma_j\)
(\(\lambda_i\ge0\)), formula \eqref{eq:Atoric} gives the linear expression
\(A_{X,\Delta}(v_w)=\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_i\beta_i\); moreover
\(\min_{u\in P}\langle u,w\rangle = \langle u_j,w\rangle
=-\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_i\beta_i\)
is also linear on \(\sigma_j\) (the minimum over the vertices is attained
at \(u_j\), because for \(k\ne j\),
\(\langle u_k,w\rangle=\langle u_j,w\rangle+\lambda_kB/a_k\ge\langle
u_j,w\rangle\)). Hence by \eqref{eq:BJS} both \(A_{X,\Delta}(v_w)\) and
\(S_L(v_w)=\langle\bary,w\rangle-\langle u_j,w\rangle\) restrict to
\emph{linear} positive functions on \(\sigma_j\setminus\{0\}\). A ratio of
two positive linear functions on the simplex
\(\mathrm{conv}(v_i: i\neq j)\) is a fractional-linear function, hence
attains its minimum at a vertex; by homogeneity of degree zero, the
infimum of \(A/S\) over \(\sigma_j\setminus\{0\}\) is attained at one of
the rays \(\R_{\ge0}v_i\), \(i\ne j\). Taking the minimum over the
finitely many maximal cones and applying Lemma \ref{lem:Sray} gives the
claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
In Setup \textup{\ref{setup}},
\[
\delta(X,\Delta)\;=\;\delta^T(X,\Delta)
\;=\;\frac{(n+1)\min_i a_i\beta_i}{B}\;\le\;1,
\]
and \(\delta(X,\Delta)\) is computed by \(\ord_{D_{i_0}}\) for any
\(i_0\) minimizing \(a_i\beta_i\). Moreover
\(\alpha(X,\Delta)=\min_i a_i\beta_i/B=\delta(X,\Delta)/(n+1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The inequality \(\delta^T\le1\) is the arithmetic-mean bound
\((n+1)\min_ia_i\beta_i\le\sum_ia_i\beta_i=B\). Suppose, for the sake of
contradiction, that \(\delta(X,\Delta)<\delta^T(X,\Delta)\). Then
\(\delta(X,\Delta)<1\), so \((X,\Delta)\) is not K-semistable, and Theorem
\ref{thm:zhuang} applies with \(G=\Aut(X,\Delta)\supseteq T\): the
threshold is computed as an infimum over \(G\)-invariant divisors \(E\)
over \(X\) that are lc places of complements. Any such \(E\) is in
particular \(T\)-invariant, hence \(\ord_E\) is a monomial valuation by
Lemma \ref{lem:invariant}, and therefore
\(A_{X,\Delta}(E)/S_L(E)\ge\delta^T(X,\Delta)\). Taking the infimum
gives \(\delta(X,\Delta)\ge\delta^T(X,\Delta)\), a contradiction. Hence
\(\delta=\delta^T\), and Proposition \ref{prop:toric} computes it.

For the alpha invariant: evaluating at the rays and using Lemma
\ref{lem:Sray}, \(\alpha(X,\Delta)\le\min_i\beta_i/T_L(\ord_{D_i})
=\min_ia_i\beta_i/B\). Conversely, by \eqref{eq:ST},
\(\alpha(X,\Delta)\ge\delta(X,\Delta)/(n+1)=\min_ia_i\beta_i/B\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:complement}
Theorem \ref{thm:zhuang} restricts the infimum to invariant divisors that
are lc places of complements; this class is nonempty and, in our setting,
contains \emph{all} toric boundary divisors: the \(\Q\)-divisor
\(D:=\sum_i\beta_iD_i\sim_\Q-(K_X+\Delta)\) satisfies
\(\Delta+D=\sum_iD_i\), the full toric boundary, which is log canonical,
and every monomial valuation is an lc place of it by
\eqref{eq:Atoric}. This is consistent with (though not needed for) the
proof above, which only uses that Zhuang's class consists of invariant
divisors.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:known}
For \(\Delta=0\) and \(X=\PP(a_0,\dots,a_n)\) the value
\(\delta=(n+1)a_{\min}/h\), \(h=\sum a_i\), can also be extracted from
\cite[Corollary 7.19]{BJ20}: the barycenter \(\bary\) of the anticanonical
simplex satisfies \(-c\,\bary\in\partial P\) exactly for
\(c=(n+1)a_{\min}/(h-(n+1)a_{\min})\) (when \(a_{\min}<h/(n+1)\)),
and \(\delta=c/(1+c)\). Similarly, for smooth toric Fano varieties
the analogous formula for the greatest Ricci lower bound goes back to
\cite{Li11}. What we wish to stress is the uniform statement for arbitrary
boundaries and arbitrary quotient lattices, and the method of proof via
equivariance, which sidesteps the initial-degeneration argument of
\cite[\S7]{BJ20} entirely.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:degenerate}
The proof shows more precisely that for \emph{any} complete simplicial
fan whose rays satisfy a positive linear relation, i.e.\ any fake weighted
projective space, K-instability of a torus-invariant pair is always
witnessed by one of the \(n+1\) boundary divisors themselves --- no
higher birational model is needed. In the language of
\cite{Zhu21,BLXZ25}, the \emph{optimal destabilizing center} of
\((X,\Delta)\) is the divisor \(D_{i_0}\) with \(a_{i_0}\beta_{i_0}\)
minimal (when the minimizer is unique), and the associated degeneration
stays within the toric category. This gives a large supply of explicitly
computable instances of the destabilization-and-replacement iteration
used in the recent birational proof of properness of K-moduli spaces
\cite{BLXZ25}.
\end{remark}

\section{Consequences}\label{sec:consequences}

\begin{corollary}[K-semistability criterion]\label{cor:kss}
In Setup \textup{\ref{setup}}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \(\delta(X,\Delta)\le1\), so \((X,\Delta)\) is never uniformly
K-stable;
\item \((X,\Delta)\) is K-semistable \(\iff\)
\(a_0\beta_0=\dots=a_n\beta_n\) \(\iff\) \(\bary=0\); in this case
\((X,\Delta)\) is K-polystable and admits a K\"ahler--Einstein metric
with cone angle \(2\pi\beta_i\) along \(D_i\);
\item for \(\Delta=0\), \(X\) is K-semistable \(\iff\)
\(a_0=\dots=a_n=1\) \(\iff\) \(X\cong\PP^n/G\) for a finite abelian
group \(G\) acting freely in codimension one, and then \(X\) is
K-polystable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(i) is part of Theorem \ref{thm:main}. For (ii): by Theorem
\ref{thm:main}, \(\delta\ge1\) iff \((n+1)\min_ia_i\beta_i\ge B\), which
by the arithmetic-mean bound happens iff all \(a_i\beta_i\) are equal; by
\eqref{eq:barypairing} this is equivalent to
\(\langle\bary,v_i\rangle=0\) for all \(i\), i.e.\ \(\bary=0\) since the
\(v_i\) span. When \(\bary=0\), the toric log Fano pair \((X,\Delta)\)
admits a (conical) K\"ahler--Einstein metric by Berman--Berndtsson
\cite{BB13}, and K\"ahler--Einstein log Fano pairs are K-polystable by
Berman \cite{Ber16}. (iii) is the case \(\beta_i\equiv1\) of (ii):
equality of the weights and \(\gcd=1\) force \(a_i\equiv1\), i.e.\
\(\sum_iv_i=0\), which is exactly the ray data of \(\PP^n\) in the
sublattice \(N'\); the multiplicity group \(N/N'\) acts freely in
codimension one by primitivity of the \(v_i\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Calibration]\label{cor:calibration}
Let \(X\) be a fake weighted projective space with weights
\((a_0,\dots,a_n)\), \(a_{\min}=\min_ia_i\). The torus-invariant
boundaries \(\Delta\) for which \((X,\Delta)\) is K-polystable are
precisely
\[
\Delta(t)=\sum_{i=0}^n\Bigl(1-\frac{t}{a_i}\Bigr)D_i,
\qquad t\in(0,a_{\min}]\cap\Q .
\]
For each such \(t\) the pair \((X,\Delta(t))\) admits a conical
K\"ahler--Einstein metric; at \(t=a_{\min}\) the divisors of minimal
weight appear with coefficient \(0\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
K-semistability holds iff \(a_i\beta_i\equiv t\) for some constant
\(t>0\), i.e.\ \(c_i=1-t/a_i\); the constraint \(c_i\in[0,1)\) is
equivalent to \(0<t\le a_{\min}\). Polystability and the metric follow
from Corollary \ref{cor:kss}(ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Lattice independence]\label{cor:lattice}
Let \(X=\PP(a_0,\dots,a_n)/G\) be a fake weighted projective space of
multiplicity \(m=[N:N']\), and let \(\Delta\), \(\Delta'\) be
corresponding torus-invariant boundaries on \(X\) and on
\(\PP(a_0,\dots,a_n)\) with the same coefficients. Then
\[
\delta(X,\Delta)=\delta\bigl(\PP(a_0,\dots,a_n),\Delta'\bigr),
\qquad
\alpha(X,\Delta)=\alpha\bigl(\PP(a_0,\dots,a_n),\Delta'\bigr),
\]
although \(\Vol(-(K_X+\Delta)) = \tfrac1m\Vol(-(K+\Delta'))\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The formulas of Theorem \ref{thm:main} depend only on
\((a_i,\beta_i)_i\), not on \(N\). The volume statement is standard:
\(\Vol(L)=n!\,\Vol_M(P)\) and passing from \(N'\) to \(N\) rescales the
dual measure by \(1/m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Corollary \ref{cor:lattice} is an instance of the general principle that
\(\delta\) is preserved under quotients by finite groups acting without
ramification divisor (compare \cite{LZ22,Zhu21}: K-polystability descends
along Galois covers); for toric quotients the equality is visibly exact at
the level of each toric valuation, since neither \eqref{eq:BJS} (after the
barycenter is expressed in weight data) nor \eqref{eq:Atoric} sees the
lattice.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Extremality for the Blum--Jonsson inequality]
\label{cor:alpha}
Every pair in Setup \textup{\ref{setup}} satisfies
\(\alpha(X,\Delta)=\delta(X,\Delta)/(n+1)\); thus the lower bound in
\(\delta/(n+1)\le\alpha\le n\delta/(n+1)\) is attained in every dimension
and for every value \(\delta\in(0,1]\cap\Q\) of the stability threshold.
Moreover the greatest Ricci lower bound satisfies
\(R(X,\Delta)=\delta(X,\Delta)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first claim is in Theorem \ref{thm:main}; every value
\(\delta=(n+1)a_{\min}\beta/B\in(0,1]\) is realized by suitable
\((a_i,\beta_i)\) (e.g.\ fix \(\beta_i\equiv1\) and vary weights, then
correct by a small boundary using density of the values of the formula).
The identity \(R=\min\{1,\delta\}\) holds for log Fano pairs by
\cite{CRZ19,BBJ21}, and \(\delta\le1\) here.
\end{proof}

\section{Examples, chambers, and wall crossing}\label{sec:examples}

\subsection{The football}\label{ss:football}
For \(X=\PP^1\), \(\Delta=c_0\{0\}+c_1\{\infty\}\), Theorem
\ref{thm:main} gives
\[
\delta = \frac{2\min(\beta_0,\beta_1)}{\beta_0+\beta_1},
\]
which is \(1\) iff \(\beta_0=\beta_1\). We recover the classical theorem of
Troyanov \cite{Tro91}: a football (spindle) with cone angles
\(2\pi\beta_0,2\pi\beta_1\) carries a constant-curvature conical metric
iff the two angles are equal --- here re-derived as a K-stability
statement.

\subsection{Weighted projective planes}\label{ss:wpp}
For \(X=\PP(a_0,a_1,a_2)\), \(\Delta=0\):
\(\delta = 3a_{\min}/(a_0+a_1+a_2)\). For instance
\[
\delta(\PP(1,1,2))=\tfrac34,\qquad
\delta(\PP(1,2,3))=\tfrac12,\qquad
\delta(\PP(2,3,5))=\tfrac35,\qquad
\delta(\PP(1,1,n))=\tfrac{3}{n+2}.
\]
In each case the threshold is computed by a weight-\(a_{\min}\)
coordinate divisor. (For \(\PP(1,2,3)\) one checks
\(\bary=(0,-\tfrac13)\) in the coordinates of Section \ref{sec:proof},
and \cite[Corollary 7.19]{BJ20} gives \(c=1\), \(\delta=c/(1+c)=\tfrac12\),
in agreement.) The calibrating boundaries of Corollary
\ref{cor:calibration} at \(t=a_{\min}\) are, respectively,
\[
\tfrac12D_z \ \text{on } \PP(1,1,2),\qquad
\tfrac12D_y+\tfrac23D_z \ \text{on } \PP(1,2,3),\qquad
\tfrac13D_y+\tfrac35D_z \ \text{on } \PP(2,3,5).
\]
Each of these pairs is K-polystable with a conical K\"ahler--Einstein
metric; none was accessible to smoothability-based techniques.

\subsection{Fake examples and lattice independence}\label{ss:fake}
Let \(\bmu_3\subset\mathrm{PGL}_3\) act on \(\PP^2\) by
\((x:y:z)\mapsto(x:\omega y:\omega^2z)\). The quotient
\(X=\PP^2/\bmu_3\) is a fake projective plane with weights \((1,1,1)\)
and multiplicity \(3\); by Corollary \ref{cor:kss}(iii) it is
K-polystable with \(\delta=1\). Similarly the quotient
\(\PP(1,1,2)/\bmu_2\) with ray data
\(v_0=(-1,2),v_1=(-1,-2),v_2=(1,0)\) in \(N=\Z^2\) has
\(\delta=\tfrac34=\delta(\PP(1,1,2))\), while its degree drops from
\(8\) to \(4\). Both computations were confirmed numerically at level
\(m\le48\) from invariant monomial bases.

\subsection{Chamber decomposition of the coefficient cube}
\label{ss:chambers}
Fix the weights and regard
\(\delta(c)=\delta(X,\textstyle\sum_ic_iD_i)\) as a function on the cube
\([0,1)^{n+1}\ni c=(c_0,\dots,c_n)\). Theorem \ref{thm:main} exhibits
\(\delta\) as the minimum of the \(n+1\) smooth functions
\(c\mapsto (n+1)a_i(1-c_i)/B(c)\), so the cube decomposes into the
closed chambers
\[
\mathsf{Ch}_i=\bigl\{c : a_i(1-c_i)\le a_j(1-c_j)\ \forall j\bigr\},
\qquad 0\le i\le n,
\]
on which the optimal destabilizing divisor is \(D_i\). The K-semistable
locus is the common intersection
\(\bigcap_i\mathsf{Ch}_i=\{c(t):0<t\le a_{\min}\}\), the calibration
segment of Corollary \ref{cor:calibration}: a line segment in an
\((n+1)\)-cube, of codimension \(n\). Figure \ref{fig:chambers}
illustrates the case of \(\PP^1\) (where by \S\ref{ss:football} weights
are \((1,1)\)); the same picture governs each two-dimensional slice of
the cube in higher dimensions.

\subsection{One-parameter wall crossing on the quadric cone}
\label{ss:quadriccone}
Let \(X=\PP(1,1,2)\), the cone over a conic, and let \(D=D_z\) be the
weight-\(2\) coordinate divisor (a smooth conic through the smooth
locus). For \(\Delta=cD\), \(c\in[0,1)\), Theorem \ref{thm:main} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cone}
\delta(c)=\frac{3\min\{1,\;2(1-c)\}}{4-2c}
=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{3}{4-2c}, & 0\le c\le\tfrac12,\\[2ex]
\dfrac{3(1-c)}{2-c}, & \tfrac12\le c<1 .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\delta\) increases from \(\tfrac34\) to the K-polystable wall
\(\delta(\tfrac12)=1\) and then decreases to \(0\); the optimal
destabilizer jumps at the wall from a weight-one divisor \(D_x\) to
\(D_z\) itself (Figure \ref{fig:wall}). The K\"ahler--Einstein pair at
the wall, \((\PP(1,1,2),\tfrac12D_z)\), is precisely the type of
non-smoothable toric pair that appears as an orbifold double-cover target
in the K-moduli wall-crossing analysis of Kim--Liu--Wang \cite{KLW24};
formula \eqref{eq:cone} gives, without any Abban--Zhuang estimate, the
exact threshold at which such toric degenerate fibers enter or leave the
K-moduli spaces \(M_w\) of \emph{loc.\ cit.}\ in the torus-invariant
locus.

\subsection{Ambient baselines for weighted hypersurfaces}
\label{ss:ambient}
Rescaling Theorem \ref{thm:main} to the fundamental divisor: for
\(X=\PP(a_0,\dots,a_{n+1})\) of dimension \(n+1\) with
\(h=\sum_ia_i\), one has \(S_{\mathcal O_X(1)}=S_{-K_X}/h\), hence
\(\delta(\mathcal O_X(1))=h\cdot\delta(-K_X)=(n+2)\,a_{\min}\).
It is suggestive to compare this
with the Sano--Tasin bound
\(\delta(\mathcal O_{X_d}(1))\ge(n+1)a_r/d\) for a quasi-smooth
hypersurface \(X_d\subset\PP(a_0,\dots,a_{n+1})\) with \(a_r\mid d\),
\(a_r>1\) \cite{ST24}: the hypersurface bound has exactly the shape of
the ambient formula, with the dimension count shifted by one and the
degree \(d\) playing the role of a weight. Theorem \ref{thm:main} shows
that this shape is forced already by the toric ambient geometry, and
suggests that the sharp constant for hypersurfaces should likewise be
governed by a barycenter datum; the plt-flag framework of \cite{CFST26}
seems well suited to test this.

\section{Questions}\label{sec:questions}

\begin{question}
Theorem \ref{thm:main} identifies the optimal destabilizing divisor of
any K-unstable torus-invariant pair on a fake weighted projective space.
The associated toric degeneration replaces \((X,\Delta)\) by a new toric
pair, and the replacement process of \cite{BLXZ25} can be iterated within
the toric category. Starting from an arbitrary \((X,\Delta)\) as in Setup
\ref{setup}, does the iteration always terminate at a calibrated pair
\((X',\Delta'(t))\) as in Corollary \ref{cor:calibration}, and in how
many steps? A closed answer would give a completely explicit instance of
the properness algorithm of \cite{BLXZ25}.
\end{question}

\begin{question}
For pairs \((X, cD)\) with \(D\) a \emph{general} member of
\(|\mathcal O_X(k)|\) rather than a torus-invariant divisor, the
threshold \(\delta(X,cD)\) is no longer given by Theorem \ref{thm:main},
but it is natural to ask whether the torus-invariant formula computes its
infimum over the linear system, i.e.\ whether toric degenerations of the
boundary are always destabilizing-optimal. A positive answer would give
sharp a priori bounds for the K-moduli wall crossings of
\cite{ADL24,KLW24} in weighted projective settings.
\end{question}

\begin{question}
Blum--Jonsson's toric formula \(\delta(L)=\lct(D_{\bary})\) holds for
every toric variety \cite{BJ20}. For which toric log Fano pairs of higher
Picard rank does \(\delta\) admit a closed form in the combinatorial data
analogous to Theorem \ref{thm:main}? The Gorenstein toric Fano threefold
tables of \cite{Yot23} suggest that the Picard-rank-one answer is the
germ of a piecewise-rational formula on the space of fans.
\end{question}
